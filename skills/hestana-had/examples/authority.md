# Responsibility and autonomy examples

All actors, delegations, tasks and observations are fictional illustrations of [responsibilities and authority](../references/responsibilities-and-authority.md). They create no delegation, task, permission or question.

## Planning is open to authorized participants

Maya is an authorized member. Her agent helps clarify an idea using context Maya may read and proposes a feature brief. It may not read another person's private session, and may not publish executable work without covered authority. If Maya can read but not write a shared task record, the agent can propose through an allowed interaction; it cannot use participation to bypass a denied write.

## A bounded publication delegation

Alex, the accountable person, accepts brief B1 revision 2. Delegation G1, written in B1, lets planner P publish prepared work implementing that scope, excluding sharing and deployment. P may move a prepared task from a backlog status to an open one and cite G1 and B1 without asking again. Discovering that shared filters would be useful does not let P publish that new scope: it captures the idea and proposes the product decision. P cannot extend G1. Unprepared work stays in backlog even with G1. If G1 is revoked, P cannot reuse it for a later action; the earlier publication stays attributed to the delegation valid then.

## Execution requirements do not grant authority

Alex owns outcome task O1 and design task H1. O1 has subtasks, so it is a group and cannot be taken; the work is taken on its subtasks. Alex gives H1 availability `HUMAN`, so only a person can take it (assigning it with a deadline). Participant N runs in a coding agent and can describe its language skills; neither that nor the runtime creates repository access, a valid assignment or permission to alter a protected area. Another member with write access to H1 may change its availability or owner, but if a person is currently holding H1, changing it to agents only contradicts the holder and is refused until it is released or reassigned. Owner and assigned are different questions: Alex stays accountable as owner while whoever takes H1 is the assigned principal.

## Asking a person, and asking whom to ask

Worker W needs a decision about a protected action and does not know who can make it. W asks Alex whom to ask; Alex names reviewer Sam. That routing does not approve the action, and Alex does not become the answerer. W then asks Sam directly. For an ordinary product question W asks the person who decides it. Only that person answers, as themselves; W's agent cannot answer for them and neither can another agent.

The question is a comment on the task that mentions Sam and awaits an answer; it stays retrievable, reaches Sam in Hestana and through a connected agent if any, is not retried or escalated, and does not prove Sam read it. It does not pause the task or end W's reservation: W works on whatever does not depend on the answer. When Sam answers, a separate prerequisite A1 remains: W checks both and stays stopped on A1, because the answer resolves only its own question.

## Technical closure and feature acceptance

Executor E delivers candidate c1 under task T1. The repository rules require a review of c1 by someone other than E. E records its checks in T1's result; the reviewer records the review as a comment on T1; E closes T1 while still assigned, which clears the assignment. No extra human click is imposed because T1 is technical work. Feature outcome O1 still needs its own integration and outcome evidence and acceptance, by Alex or an explicit delegate for O1. Neither E's work on T1 nor P's delegation G1 makes either of them O1's acceptor. If O1 promises live usability and only a local candidate is verified, O1 stays unsatisfied; accepting a candidate under a narrower contract cannot silently narrow O1. The release operator still needs the actual release authority and operational evidence.
